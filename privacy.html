Privacy policy

Athena — last updated 27 August 2026

Who this covers

Athena is a personal assistant operated by one individual for their own use. It has a single user, who is also its operator and the owner of every Google account it connects to. It does not serve, onboard, or collect data about anyone else.

What Google data is accessed

  • Google Calendar — events in the current day, to summarise the schedule.
  • Gmail — recent message metadata and content, to flag messages that change the day's priorities.

Access is granted by the account owner through Google OAuth and can be withdrawn at any time.

How it is used

The data is used for exactly one purpose: producing a start-of-day summary for the account owner. It is not used for advertising, profiling, analytics, training machine-learning models, or any purpose beyond that summary.

Where it goes

  • The owner's own computer. The assistant runs locally. OAuth tokens and session history are stored on that machine, in the owner's home directory, and are not uploaded anywhere.
  • A language model provider. To write the summary, the relevant calendar entries and message excerpts are sent to a third-party language model API (currently OpenAI) over an encrypted connection, and are subject to that provider's data handling terms.
  • The owner's private messaging channel. The finished summary is delivered to a messaging account belonging to the owner.

Data is never sold, rented, published, or shared with any other party.

Retention

Google data is fetched at the moment a summary is produced and is not stored in any database. Local session logs may retain the text of a summary until the owner deletes them. Deleting the local Hermes directory removes all stored tokens and history.

Limited use

Athena's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Revoking access

The account owner can revoke Athena's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which immediately and permanently ends all data access.
